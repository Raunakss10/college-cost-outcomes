Does the Cost of College Translate into Better Outcomes?

Dhruv Mittal, Raunak Saluja, Thalesh Gupta

Stanford lists a price of $92.9K a year. Your average Stanford student pays $12.3K. A UCSD student pays $14.8K.

What does college cost you, and what do you get back? We plotted 1,682 four-year schools from the U.S. Department of Education College Scorecard to answer this.

Cost vs. earnings 10 years after enrolling

Prototype writeup

What have we done so far?

We cleaned the College Scorecard file down to 1,682 schools. Each school awards mostly bachelor's degrees and reports both cost and earnings 10 years after entry. We kept nine columns: name, state, type, sticker cost, net price, earnings, debt, graduation rate, and enrollment. We built the main D3 scatterplot of cost against earnings, colored by school type. You switch between sticker price and net price, and the dots slide to the new position. You filter by school type with checkboxes. You search for your school, and the chart labels the dot and dims the rest. You hover over any dot to see cost, earnings, debt, graduation rate, and three ranks: value, cost, and earnings. Labels mark UCSD and Stanford so you see the gap between listed price and paid price.

What will be the most challenging part to design, and why?

The chart holds 1,682 dots, and most sit between $20K and $40K a year. Overlap hides schools in this range, so we plan to add zoom or a brush. The value metric needs work. Earnings divided by net price jumps past 100 for schools with a net price near $0, so CUNY Baruch ranks above MIT. We plan to test earnings minus four years of net price as a fairer measure. Linked charts for debt and graduation rate need to update with your selection, and we need clean state handling across views. We also need scroll steps to walk you through the and-but-therefore story with short text.

Data: U.S. Department of Education, College Scorecard, Most Recent Institution-Level Data. Earnings are median earnings of students 10 years after entry. Net price is the average annual cost after grants and scholarships.
